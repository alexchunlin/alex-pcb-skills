# Roadmap after phase 6 (2026-11-01)

What the evidence says to build next, in order, and the longer bets to try after. Every item names the finding that
motivates it and the measurement that decides whether it worked. Standing decisions (code is the source of truth, Zener
front end, 4 layers by default, Opus 5.5 at medium as the designer, Fable at default as the reviewer) are not reopened
here.

## Where we are

| | Rung 2, attempt 1 (hand routing) | Rung 2, attempt 2 (`pcb route auto`) |
|---|---|---|
| scores (elec / manuf / doc) | 8 / 6 / 7 | 8 / 8 / 7 |
| designer wall / output tokens | 38.7 min / 189k | 71.3 min / 196k |
| routing phase | 8 DRC iterations, most of the run | 1 clean call at minute 26, then 30 min of reaction |
| majors | VM pin on a pour neck, bulk caps far away, header part mismatch | header pin on plane spokes, bulk cap on a via chain, no LDO copper |

The router works (P6-T3: clean on both finished boards in under a minute, half the designers' vias) and the score
moved. The time did not, because the router was allowed to make decisions that are rules, and the designer spent its
time undoing them (R2A2-T1). The next steps turn those rules into code.

## Next steps, in order

1. **Staged `pcb route auto`** (R2A2-T1, P6-T3). One call becomes a pipeline with a report per stage: fan out the
   fine-pitch parts first (KRT's `qfn_fanout.py`), refuse to route any net in a wide class (those belong to the
   layout script, which the wrapper says by name), route each IC with its decoupling caps as a group, route the rest
   on the outer layers only, then stitch, then the dual DRC. Decides: the route bench on the four archived rung-2
   placements, staged against one-shot, clean rate and the reviewer's `feeds` rows; and attempt 3's routing phase in
   minutes (target under 10 of a sub-40-minute run).
2. **Generated `feeds` rows** (R2A2-O-1, O-2, R2-O-2, R2-S-1). Every pin of a net in a wide class gets a `feeds` row
   at the class current without the designer declaring it. Three of the last five electrical majors were a wide net
   reaching a pin through something no row counted. Decides: the replay bench, the three rung-2 majors caught in the
   `designer` scope, 0 false fails.
3. **Rules into pcblib, out of thinking** (R2A2-T1, the 234-second thermal-pad generation). Helpers with a check row
   behind each: `gnd_via_at(pads)`, `bypass(cap, pin)` placing the cap on the pin side with its GND via, `plane(layer,
   net)` as the inner-layer default, `wide_net(net)` drawing the feed with the class width and the vias the `feeds` row
   wants. Decides: designer thinking tokens and the count of long generations on attempt 3 against 107k and 3.
4. **The rows the rung-2 reviews asked for** (P1-T22, R2A2-O-3): pour-neck width by polygon erosion, a sourcing
   attributes row (JLC part pins against footprint pads), capacitor ripple-current rating, bring-up text against the
   pin map, LDO pour area against the quoted theta. Decides: the replay bench, each row catching its labelled finding.
5. **Rung-2 attempt 3** with items 1 to 3 in place, same prompt, Opus at medium, reviewer Fable at default. The
   hypothesis from attempt 2 again, now with a tool that stays off the planes: scores at or above 8/8/7, wall time
   under 40 minutes, routing phase under 10. One run each side is weak evidence, so attempt 3 runs twice if the first
   result is within 20 percent of attempt 2 on time.
6. **Ordering search** (P6-T6). The router is deterministic and a run is 20 to 40 seconds, so route five net orderings
   in parallel and keep the one with zero errors and the fewest vias. Decides: the route bench, vias and length against
   the single run; it has to beat a one-line sort to stay.
7. **The design-margin experiment** (GAMEPLAN phase 6). Same rung-2 netlist at 4, 6 and 8 layers, at +20 percent area,
   at 0603 against 0402, on the route bench as it stands. Decides which margin knob the loop turns first and whether
   layers earn their default. This is the experiment that settles the 4-versus-6 question Alex leaned on.
8. **The per-sheet loop** (session-2 handoff): one sub-agent per Zener module with a sheet contract and `pcb check
   --only` on its rows before hand-back; layout stays one agent. Agent tool unless Alex says "use a workflow".

## Moonshot R&D, later

Each has a prior-art check and a cheap first experiment. None starts before items 1 to 5 above, because the bench
numbers say the time is in rules, not in the router.

- **Placement search with routing as the judge.** The router's result is bounded by where the parts sit, and open
  placement has no benchmark (GAMEPLAN). With a 40-second router, score placement candidates by what they route to:
  clean, vias, length, the `feeds` and `decoupling` rows. Prior art: KRT's `place_simple.py` with `route.py --quick`
  as the seed judge, kct's annealer. First experiment: 12 candidate placements of the rung-2 board from the designer's
  floor plan, routed and scored, against the designer's own. If the best candidate beats it on the reviewer rows, the
  loop gets a placement stage.
- **Topological routing.** Strings in space that choose which side of each obstacle a net passes, collapsed onto layers
  afterwards: rubber-band routing from the 1980s, Topola today (no release). It plans where grid routers are weak. Watch
  Topola; the first experiment is the route bench on the Opus board the day it reads a KiCad file.
- **A System One dispatcher.** Jev (typesafe.ai) returns a typed decision with a calibrated probability in 70 to 500 ms.
  Not a router: step-by-step direction choice by a model is the measured worst option (PCBWorld, zero medium boards
  clean) and no speed-up turns a classifier into a backtracking search. As the per-net dispatcher it fits: features
  from `pcb q` in, one of "hand-draw, outer layers, plane" out, below-threshold confidence escalates to the slow model.
  Prior art: a rule table. First experiment: Jev against the rules on the four rung-2 placements; if the table does as
  well, the table wins. Needs early-access API keys.
- **Learned routing policies.** PCBWorld's PPO policy beat freerouting 0.86 to 0.80 on small real boards. A policy
  that reads KRT's obstacle map and proposes net order and layer per net, with KRT doing the geometry, is the shape
  that could pay. Prior art: KRT's ordering strategies (item 6 measures them first). Not before the ordering search
  shows there is spread to learn from.
- **Reviewer as a bench, not a judge.** The reviewer re-measures everything and its majors become rows within a day.
  The moonshot is closing the loop: every reviewer finding that is a measurement becomes a row automatically (the
  finding's evidence line is already a `pcb q` or `pcb check` command), so the review skill emits `review_checks.json`
  rows the designer's next attempt must pass. Prior art: `review_checks.json` exists; the missing step is the harvest.
- **Six and eight layers as a routing margin, not a cost.** If item 7 shows 6 layers halves vias and removes the plane
  conflicts, the plane policy becomes the router's constraint set and the default changes. That is Alex's lean; the
  experiment decides it.

## What does not get built

- An LLM in the routing loop choosing directions. Measured worst (PCBWorld), and the router is already under a minute.
- A replacement router. KRT is deterministic, reads KiCad files, and routed clean where freerouting could not; the gaps
  found so far (order sensitivity, plane use, wide nets) are configuration and staging, which is the smaller change.
- More review rows before the staged router. The last two attempts' majors came from the router's choices; fix the
  cause first, then label what remains.
